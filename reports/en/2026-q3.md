We, a community based in Taiwan, collect two kinds of observation data every day. The [Tor Relay Watch](/projects/pulse/) tracks how many volunteer-run Tor (onion routing network) relays operate in Taiwan and nearby regions, and [OONI Coverage](/projects/asn-coverage/) tracks which networks the censorship measurements of OONI (Open Observatory of Network Interference) come from. Both pages show the last 60 days. Every three months the quarterly report fixes the numbers in place, looks at the quarter as a whole, and adds our reading of them. This is the first report, covering 1 July to 30 September 2026.

<!-- region-map -->

## Highlights

- Taiwan had 11 running Tor relays at the end of the quarter, two fewer than at the start. That is about 0.4 relays per million internet users, the same as South Korea, against 3.2 in Hong Kong and 15 in Singapore
- 8 of the 11 relays run on Chunghwa Telecom's HiNet (AS3462), and the top two networks carry 82% of them, the highest concentration among the ten regions we track
- Two Tor releases appeared in September, and by the end of the quarter seven in ten relays in Taiwan had upgraded, eighth among the ten regions
- OONI recorded about 2.76 million measurements in Taiwan this quarter, and the networks with measurements cover 95% of internet users. Mobile networks serve 45% of users and produced 4% of the measurements
- The community's OONI Run link `10328` sent 39,116 measurements this quarter, 2.2 times the quarter before

## Tor relays

<!-- rq-relays-stats -->

Taiwan started the quarter with 13 running relays and ended it with 11. Observed bandwidth fell from 60 MB/s to 57 MB/s, and the number of exit relays held at four. The chart below includes the previous quarter, and the count stayed between 10 and 16 throughout.

<!-- rq-relays-chart -->

Pulse stopped collecting from 8 July to 23 August, so the blank stretch in the chart is missing data rather than relays going offline. 45 of the quarter's 92 days have data. The comparison between the start and the end of the quarter is unaffected, but this report gives no quarterly averages.

### Relays that joined and left

<!-- rq-churn -->

Of the 13 relays running at the start of the quarter, 10 were still running at the end and 3 had left, while 1 new relay had joined. 16 relays appeared at some point during the quarter, and 7 of them were running on at least nine in ten of the 45 days with data. With numbers this small, one or two relays coming or going moves the total noticeably.

### Upgrades to new releases

<!-- rq-upgrade -->

Two Tor releases appeared this quarter. Version 0.4.9.12 was first seen among the ten regions on 8 September, and 0.4.9.13 on 23 September. A relay in Taiwan was running 0.4.9.12 on the day it first appeared, and by the end of the quarter 7 of Taiwan's 11 relays ran one of the two. That ranks eighth among the ten regions, while Vietnam and Hong Kong were both above 85%. New Tor releases usually carry security fixes, and operators can subscribe to the Tor Project's tor-announce mailing list to hear about them as soon as they ship.

### Network distribution

<!-- rq-asn -->

Taiwan's 11 relays at the end of the quarter ran on four networks: 8 on HiNet, and one each on the Taiwan Academic Network (TANet), Taiwan Digital Streaming and Hoshin Multimedia. The top two networks carry 82%, the highest of the ten regions, against 23% in Japan. With relays concentrated on one provider, an outage at that provider, or pressure on it, takes most of them down together. [Tor relays on campus](/join/relay-on-campus/) puts relays on the academic network, which is one way to spread them out.

### Comparison with nearby regions

<!-- rq-density -->

Taiwan has 0.4 relays per million internet users, the same as South Korea and slightly fewer than Japan's 0.5. Hong Kong and Singapore have far fewer internet users, so their relay counts work out higher per person. The Netherlands and Germany host more relays than anywhere else and are shown for reference.

## OONI measurement coverage

<!-- rq-ooni-stats -->

OONI recorded about 2.76 million measurements in Taiwan this quarter, a fifth more than the quarter before, and the number of networks with measurements rose from 35 to 44. By the estimates of APNIC (the Asia Pacific Network Information Centre), the networks measured cover 95% of internet users, about the same as last quarter. The measurements also became more concentrated, with the top two networks' share rising from 53% to 65%, mostly because HiNet went from 28% to 44%. The second-largest network accounts for 0.05% of users but a fifth of the measurements, which suggests test devices running automatically around the clock.

### By network type

<!-- rq-types -->

All three mobile networks had measurements this quarter, so their coverage is 100%, but the volume differs widely. Mobile networks serve 45% of users and produced 4.3% of the measurements. Chunghwa Telecom's mobile network alone serves 17% of users, and produced 0.3% of the measurements on 50 days of the quarter. The "other" group serves 2.6% of users yet produced nearly 40% of the measurements, and includes fibre providers with very few users as well as cloud, hosting and VPN networks. OONI assigns a country by IP address, so measurements sent through a VPN exit in Taiwan count toward Taiwan.

Network types are tagged by hand from network names and public information, and only networks with at least 0.1% of users whose type we could confirm are tagged.

### Networks with no measurements this quarter

<!-- rq-gaps -->

Nine networks with at least 0.1% of users had no measurements at all this quarter, about 3.4% of users combined. All three ASNs of UNION BROADBAND NETWORK (UBB) are on the list, and one of them, AS24164, had 494 measurements last quarter and none this quarter.

### Changes in the networks measured

<!-- rq-changes -->

Of the 18 networks measured this quarter but not last, the cable providers, So-net, Taiwan Fixed Network and National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University are local networks. Most of the rest are hosting and VPN networks.

### Daily measurements

<!-- rq-ooni-daily -->

The busiest day was 13 August, with 47,042 measurements. That day seven counties and cities in northern Taiwan held a mobile-network throttling drill, and the community asked people to record it with OONI Probe (see [the drill results](docs:blog/2026/08/ooni-mobile-throttle-drill-results/) on the docs site).

Of the 2.35 million website tests (Web Connectivity) this quarter, 21 were marked as confirmed blocking, spread across seven domains and five networks, with at most eight on any one domain. Nothing points to blocking concentrated on a particular network. Domains with a high anomaly rate were mostly anomalous on every network, which looks more like the site itself being unreachable. [How OONI decides a site is blocked](docs:community/ooni-blocking-determination/) explains how to read such results.

### The community OONI Run link

<!-- rq-run -->

The community's OONI Run link `10328` sent 39,116 measurements this quarter, 2.2 times the 17,518 of the quarter before and 1.4% of all measurements in Taiwan. Nine tenths came from Taiwan Mobile's mobile network, while Chunghwa Telecom's mobile network and Far EasTone together sent fewer than 200.

## Events this quarter

- 8 July to 23 August: Pulse stopped collecting for 47 days
- 13 August: mobile-network throttling drill in seven northern counties and cities, the day with the most OONI measurements this quarter
- 8 and 23 September: Tor 0.4.9.12 and 0.4.9.13 first seen in the regions we track

## Help needed next quarter

- **OONI measurements on mobile networks**: Chunghwa Telecom's mobile network and Far EasTone together serve three in ten users and produced under 1% of the measurements. Install [OONI Probe](https://ooni.org/install/mobile) on a phone using mobile data and open the community's OONI Run link `10328`. The [OONI Run v2 guide](docs:tools/ooni-run-v2/) covers the risks to weigh first
- **Cable broadband**: UNION BROADBAND NETWORK, VEE TIME and New Kaohsiung Broadband had no measurements all quarter, and a home connection on any of them adds a network
- **Tor relays outside HiNet**: university networks and other providers both help; see [how to set up a Tor relay](docs:community/setup-tor-relay/) and [Tor relays on campus](/join/relay-on-campus/)
- **Relay upgrades**: if you run a relay, move to 0.4.9.13 or later

## Data and method

- The period is 1 July to 30 September 2026 (UTC), compared with 1 April to 30 June
- Tor relay data comes from [Pulse](https://github.com/anoni-net/pulse), run by the community, which collects from Onionoo, part of Tor Metrics, every hour, and each day uses its last snapshot. The start and end of the quarter are the snapshots of 1 July and 30 September, and "running on nine in ten days" counts only the 45 days with data
- The snapshot of 7 April captured only a few relays and is treated as missing data
- A release's first-seen date is the first day it appeared in any of the ten regions, which is not necessarily the Tor Project's release date
- OONI figures come from [OONI's aggregation API](https://api.ooni.io/) and include every test type
- Users per network are [APNIC's estimates](https://stats.labs.apnic.net/aspop/) as of 8 October 2026. APNIC only publishes its latest estimate, so this quarter and the previous one use the same figures, and changes in coverage reflect only changes in which networks were measured
- The network type tags are in [`data/reports/asn-types.toml`](https://github.com/anoni-net/www/blob/main/data/reports/asn-types.toml), and the raw numbers behind every chart in this report are in [`data/reports/2026-q3.json`](https://github.com/anoni-net/www/blob/main/data/reports/2026-q3.json)
