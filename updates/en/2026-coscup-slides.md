On 8 and 9 August, anoni.net ran a two-day community track at COSCUP 2026. Its 16 sessions went from how the internet works, threat models and open-source privacy tools to campus Tor relays, browser tracking, data-privacy rights and anonymous payments. Since then speakers have shared their slides, and slides for 11 sessions plus one recording now sit under each session on the [track page](/events/coscup-2026/#schedule-overview), for anyone who missed the day or wants to look something up again.

Each set of slides has a one-line summary below so you can pick what to open. Copyright stays with each speaker, so please ask the speaker before reposting or adapting their slides unless a license is noted. Most talks were in Mandarin, and so are most of the slides.

[Go to the track page](/events/coscup-2026/#schedule-overview){ .btn .solid }

## Introduction to open-source anonymity networks, 8 August morning

**Meet Anonymity Networks Community: open-source anonymity tools, community practice, and the three 2026 themes**  
anoni.net community · [Slides (PDF, 14 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/anoni-net-intro.pdf){target="_blank"}

How the community translates Tor, Tails and OONI and runs relays, where Taiwan falls short on anonymous whistleblowing and Tor relays, and how to take part without a technical background.

**Threat models and metadata 101: know your adversary, and why anonymity tools must be open source to be trustworthy**  
anoni.net community · [Slides (PDF, 20 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/anoni-net-threat-model.pdf){target="_blank"}

An independent journalist and an employee passing on documents walk through three questions (what to protect, from whom, at what cost), followed by why metadata is often more dangerous than content.

**Real-world open-source privacy tools: how civil-society groups, independent journalists, and individuals actually use them**  
anoni.net community · [Slides (PDF, 16 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/anoni-net-realworld-tools.pdf){target="_blank"}

Open-source tools such as Signal, CryptPad, OnionShare and mat2 for civil society, journalists and individuals, each marked with how hard it is to pick up and where it falls short.

## Anonymous payments with ETHTaipei, 8 August afternoon

**"I don't launder money — so why understand anonymous payments?" A from-scratch intro to private crypto flows**  
mashbean · [Slides (web)](https://mashbean.net/decks/coscup-2026-privacy-money/){target="_blank"}

From anti-money-laundering rules to public crypto ledgers, comparing how Tornado Cash, Privacy Pools and Railgun trade privacy against keeping out dirty money. No crypto background needed.

**Hands-on private payments workshop: from Tornado Cash to Privacy Pool**  
Liangcc · [Slides (web)](https://chihchengliang.github.io/notes/2026-07-27_privacy_pools/slides.html){target="_blank"}

Tornado Cash and Privacy Pools compared, with steps from installing a wallet to depositing and withdrawing, which you can practise on a testnet simulation.

## Accepted talks, 9 August

**The Workings of the Internet: how the net works and how censors block you (in English)**  
Raghu · [Slides (PDF, 141 pages, 6.7 MB)](https://saxrag.com/bucket/random/workings_of_the_internet.pdf){target="_blank"}

Postcards as the analogy for a connection, from DNS to HTTPS: what your ISP and the café Wi-Fi can each see and block. In English.

**Building a home network with OpenWRT and other open-source software**  
Pellaeon · [Slides (PDF, 23 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/pellaeon-openwrt-home-network.pdf){target="_blank"}

A router under NT$3,000 running OpenWRT gives a home an isolated guest network, several public IP addresses and a site-to-site VPN, with buying advice.

**Growing onions on campus? Running an academic Tor node at NTNU and lessons from the EFF Tor University Challenge**  
NZ · [Slides (PDF, 19 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/nz-tor-relay-on-campus.pdf){target="_blank"} · [Recording (Google Drive)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1bAzgU6rSNg9hXSrDhDIXYd4PM-P8Dxte/view?usp=sharing){target="_blank"}

The technical setup and administrative route behind a student-run Tor relay at NTNU, and what is still unsolved, such as power cuts and handing over after graduation. There is also a recording of this talk.

**Browser tracking, anti-tracking strategies, and user autonomy**  
Pellaeon · [Slides (PDF, 32 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/pellaeon-browser-tracking-and-user-autonomy.pdf){target="_blank"}

Brave's randomisation and Tor Browser's uniformity compared as anti-tracking strategies, plus separating your browsing across several Firefox profiles.

**After the health-insurance database case: exercising the right to stop secondary use, and other large databases**  
Kuan-Ju Chou · [Slides (PDF, 18 pages)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/kuan-nhi-database-right-to-opt-out.pdf){target="_blank"}

Ten years after the lawsuit over Taiwan's health insurance database, people can apply to stop secondary use from August 2025; the second half surveys other large government databases such as school records, labour insurance and e-invoices.

**Privacy guide 2026**  
Justyn · [Slides (PDF, 40 pages, 19 MB)](https://assets.anoni.net/event/coscup-2026/justyn-privacy-guides-2026.pdf){target="_blank"}

Judge how much protection your situation needs, then choose messaging, payments and devices accordingly, ending with one 15-minute improvement for each level.
